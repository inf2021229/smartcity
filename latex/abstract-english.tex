In this thesis, a complete reporting platform for smart cities is designed, developed, and evaluated, which seeks to improve the way that citizens communicate with the municipality authorities by registering urban problems. The platform aims to overcome the weaknesses of the traditional mechanisms, such as phone calls and in person visits in municipal services, that often lead to insufficient information, delays, and difficulty tracking the progress of a request. In contrast to these services, a new digital solution is being proposed that helps to easily document these infrastructure problems and offers a way to process them through a single and expandable digital structure. The system consists of two independent but interconnected functional parts. The main part is the mobile application which lets users submit reports on the spot, taking advantage of the functions of their device like using the camera and geolocation. In addition to the mobile app, a website application that is used mainly by the authorities handles the processing and visualization of these reports. The users of the mobile app can make a report by taking a picture of the problem, writing a description, and sending it with its coordinates. Respectively, the admins of the website have access to all the reports which are displayed in an interactive map so that both sides can track their progress and can be changed in the admin dashboard page. This project covers the requirements, architecture, and implementation of such a system. In a technical perspective, the platform was developed following a three layered structure that helps to easily maintain, expand, and manage each functionality alone. The backend side was made with Node.js and Express and works as a communication bridge between the applications and the database. All the data are stored in a cloud database using MongoDB Atlas through the database. The mobile app was implemented with Ionic React, while the web application visualizes the reports on a map using the open source library Leaflet. The final product was evaluated using a questionnaire with 27 participants, who were asked to look at the system and fill out a form with answers on a Likert scale ranging from one to five. The participants stated that they thought of the system as useful and easily understandable, while not hiding the fact that they would use such a platform if it was available. The result showcased a high level of satisfaction, strong positive response and indication that the system could fill the existing void found in urban problem management. In general, the thesis shows how a modern, lightweight and technologically accessible platform can contribute in strengthening smart cities, reducing the cost and time of resolving problems and increasing the participation of citizens. Lastly, the system’s functional base can be easily expanded in the future with new additions that will greatly impact the effectiveness and the performance of it.
